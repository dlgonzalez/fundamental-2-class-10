03 · Listening · Student's Book p. 50

What's in Your Neighborhood?

Use the coursebook audio 5.04. The teacher provides and plays the audio. No audio is embedded in this learning plan.

A · PRE-LISTENING

Look and predict

Look at the two neighborhood pictures in the book: colorful houses and a city with tall buildings.

  • Would you like to live in each place?
  • What places can you see or imagine there?
  • Which neighborhood looks quieter / busier?
B · FIRST LISTENING

Listen for specific information

Play Track 5.04. Students check the places Elsa and Ahmed mention in the book's table.

  • coffee shop
  • park · gym · bus stop
  • train station · grocery store
  • library · apartment building · high school
C · SECOND LISTENING

Who likes their neighborhood?

  1. Play the audio again.
  2. Students complete / check the table for Elsa and Ahmed.
  3. Students decide who likes their neighborhood and identify the details that support their answer.
  4. Pair-check before whole-class feedback.
D · THINKING SKILL – EVALUATE

Where would you prefer to live?

Discuss the book questions:

  • What are the differences between living outside a town or city and living close to the city?
  • Where do you like to live? Why?
Useful language: I prefer… because… · There are more… · It is quieter / busier… · There isn't… · There are…
Teacher note: Play the official Track 5.04 from your course materials. Keep the listening stages separate: first identify places, then listen again for the speakers' opinions and evidence.

04 · Group listening challenge

How do people feel about their cities?

Students work in small groups with the external listening resource. The focus is listening for opinions and specific descriptive details.

🎧🌍
GROUP WORK

Places – Listening Practice

Match descriptions to cities, listen for people's opinions and identify words that describe places.

Open listening activity ↗

Suggested group procedure

1
Getting ready

Groups read the city descriptions and match them with the cities. They discuss which clues helped them.

2
Listening 1 – people's opinions

Listen and decide whether each speaker likes their city a lot, a little or doesn't like it.

3
Listening 2 – describing a city

Listen for adjectives such as great, safe, quiet, crowded, noisy, lovely and large. Groups compare their answers.

4
Report back

Each group shares one city description and one piece of evidence they heard.

Optional extension if time allows: Listening 3 compares pairs of cities. Students can identify which city each speaker prefers and the reasons for the preference.
Teacher note: The webpage contains several listening tasks. Choose the tasks that fit the available time and level. Students should listen to the audio on the website; do not replace it with a read-aloud transcript.
15–20 min
05 · Main speaking project

RENT IT! – The Neighborhood Rental Fair

Students become real-estate agents. They explore rental advertisements, analyze descriptions, create their own property listing and present it to possible renters.

🏠🔑
SPEAKING + WRITING SUPPORT

RENT A HOUSE OR APARTMENT

Read four rental listings, complete comprehension challenges, build an advertisement and persuade the class to rent your property.

Open Rent It! activity ↗

Learning sequence

1
Explore the listings

Students read four different property advertisements. They notice prices, rooms, nearby places, adjectives and advantages / disadvantages.

2
Rental detective

Students answer the comprehension questions. Ask them to find evidence in the advertisements rather than guess.

3
Language kit

Review the useful expressions and model advertisement. Highlight there is / there are, location phrases and adjectives.

4
Create your advertisement

Pairs or teams invent a property and complete the guided builder. They should include a property name, price, rooms, at least three nearby places, five adjectives, advantages / disadvantages and an ideal renter.

5
The rental fair

Teams present their property. The audience acts as possible renters and asks questions about the home and neighborhood.

Language targets

  • There is a… / There are…
  • There isn't a… / There aren't any…
  • Is there…? / Are there any…?
  • It is located near…
  • There is a… next to / opposite…
  • It is spacious, cozy, bright…

Presentation questions

  • Is there a supermarket nearby?
  • Are there any parks in the area?
  • How many bedrooms are there?
  • Is the neighborhood quiet?
  • How much is the rent?
  • Is there a bus stop nearby?
Teacher tip: Give students enough planning time before presenting. Encourage them to use the sample advertisement as a model, not copy it. During the fair, ask the audience to choose the property they would rent and justify their decision.
35–45 min
06 · Wrap-up & reflection

Find your perfect place

Bring the lesson together with a short spoken reflection.

Exit speaking task

Students complete the sentence and share with a partner or the class:

“I would like to live in ______ because there is ______ and there are ______. The neighborhood is ______ and ______.”

Quick self-check

Final transition: “Today we explored neighborhoods, listened to different opinions and advertised a place to live. Next time, we'll continue using these descriptions to talk about places and our preferences.”
